Faculty Perspectives on Mathematics Requirements for CSE Students

Summary of 30 responses provided by Michigan CSE faculty at the faculty retreat in May 2026.

Each topic had four ordered response options:

  1. 1Not essential
  2. 2Essential for all CS grads, but not for my class
  3. 3Need before my class; transfer credit acceptable
  4. 4Need before my class; prefer Michigan Math credit

For this report, topics are grouped into four categories:

  • Single-variable calculus
  • Multivariable calculus
  • Linear algebra
  • Statistics

Other Math Competencies

Summary of the 10 written responses on the first page.

Reasoning and rigor

Proofs, logical and equational reasoning, abstraction, and expressing real problems mathematically.

Discrete mathematics

Graph theory, number theory, combinatorics, and inductive reasoning about programs.

Modeling and optimization

Multidimensional reasoning, optimization, mathematical modeling, and interpolation.

Computational methods

Numerical methods and gradient descent; one response stressed separating single-variable integration from partial derivatives.

Changes in Response to Generative AI

Summary of the 10 written responses on the second page.

Make AI and ML foundational

Add a core ML or agentic-development course and teach how modern AI systems work.

Strengthen software engineering

Put more weight on debugging, testing, reviewing, and modifying generated code.

Teach specification and communication

Emphasize formal and informal specification, technical writing, reading, and reasoning.

Address ethics and responsible use

Teach AI impacts, values, self-control, and how to use AI as a tool rather than a crutch.

Revisit prerequisites

Several comments favored stronger linear algebra and probability preparation for ML.

Preserve foundations

Change courses deliberately; avoid overcorrecting or losing fundamentals that remain important.
